The slug is embedded in every derived resource name, so its maximum length comes from the
tightest downstream limit. The template enforces `@maxLength(12)`.

## The budgets

| Derived name | Formula | Azure/GCP limit | Slug ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key Vault | `kv-<slug>-<8-char suffix>` | 24 (globally unique DNS name) | 12 |
| Storage account | `st<slug><8-char suffix>` | 24, no hyphens | 14 |
| GCP service account | `registry-reader-<slug>` | 30 | 14 |
| Container App | `dashboard-backend-<slug>` | 32 | 14 |

Key Vault is the binding constraint. The 8-character suffix is
`take(uniqueString(subscription().id), 8)`; it exists because Key Vault and storage
account names are unique across all of Azure, and another Azure customer holding
`kv-<slug>` would otherwise break the deploy.

Two of these limits surfaced during the littlebrolly launch, which used a 12-character
slug. The GCP service-account prefix had to change from `azure-registry-reader-` (22
characters, giving 34 total against a cap of 30) to `registry-reader-`. Separately,
Temporal Cloud rejected an em-dash in a service-account description string; its
`printasciiws` validation accepts printable ASCII only.

## Why the hostname is tied to the slug

The worker derives `dashboard.<slug>.withspinal.com` from the slug in six places: the
composed env URLs, the Auth0 callback wiring, the TLS certificate, the DNS record, the
smoke test, and the workflow's result payload. There is no separate hostname setting. If
the customer-facing name matters, pick the slug for the hostname and accept longer
internal resource names.
