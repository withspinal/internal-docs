A Spine receipt application is one row saying which receipt settled which installment,
for how much, on what date. SX.e has no such row. The application is the installment
itself, after it was paid. This is the hardest part of the mapping.

## The application is the paid installment

In Spine terms, when a payment is applied, SX.e takes the open `invoice_installments`
row, marks it closed, and writes the payment onto it. One SX.e row becomes both our
closed installment and our application.

| Spine column on `receipt_applications` | Where SX.e wrote it on the paid row |
|---|---|
| `amount_applied` | `aret.paymtamt` |
| `discount_taken` | `aret.discamt` |
| `application_date` | `aret.paymtdt` |
| `installment_id` | The row itself |
| `transaction_id` | The document with the same `invno` and `invsuf` |
| The receipt | `aret.checknopaid`, the cheque, and `aret.crjrnlno` and `aret.crsetno`, the posting journal |
| `gl_date` | `sasj.postdt` of that posting journal |

Both public schemas have these columns. The names match the screen labels **Paid Check
#** and **Applied Journal #**, but no Infor document names the columns, so confirm them
on the customer's data.

## Finding the receipt

The Spine wants `receipt_id` on every application. Whether SX.e gives us a foreign key
depends on one setting, **Allow AR Payment Reversals**.

| Setting | How the application finds its receipt |
|---|---|
| On | A foreign key. `aret.payrowpointer` on the paid row points at the payment record in `aretp` |
| Off | A match on values. The paid row's cheque number and posting journal equal the check record's `checkno`, `jrnlno`, and `setno`, for the same customer |

The setting only covers payments entered after it was switched on, so most customers
need both methods. The matching method has edge cases: cash payments all share cheque
number zero, so the journal has to carry the match.

Lockbox payments may have a third source. A public dictionary lists `arbcl`, with one
row per invoice a lockbox cheque paid, holding the invoice number, sequence, and amount
applied. That is a true application table, for lockbox only.

## A partial payment closes the installment

In the Spine, a partial payment leaves the installment open with a lower `balance_due`.
SX.e closes the installment at the amount paid and creates new installments for the
rest.

Infor's sample: a payment of 200.00 against invoice `871-00` for 337.49.

| Installment | Status | `balance_due` | Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Closed | 0 | 200.00 |
| 2 | Open | 68.75 | |
| 3 | Open | 68.74 | |

We never see `balance_due` fall. We see the installment close and two new ones appear.
The application points at the closed one.

## Not every closed installment was paid

A split, a credit, and a reversal also close an installment. Infor's activity report
prints which it was, as `(CK 1042)`, `(CR Applied)`, or `(Reversed)`, but the field that
stores it is not documented. Write an application only for installments closed by a
payment. This is the first thing to confirm on the customer's data.

## Discounts and write-offs

`discount_taken` is `aret.discamt`. SX.e puts earned and late discounts in the same
field. The discount date, `aret.discdt`, against the pay date tells them apart.

A short payment that the clerk wrote off becomes an `ar_adjustments` row on the
invoice, negative, with the amount from `aret.pifamt`. SX.e has no reason code or
approval step for a write-off, so the row lands with `status = approved`.

We do not know whether `aret.paymtamt` includes the discount. `amount_applied` must not.

## funding_source

The kind of row that funded the application decides the value.

| What funded the application | `funding_source` |
|---|---|
| A payment applied in the same posting | `cash` |
| Unapplied cash from an overpayment | `overpayment` |
| Unapplied cash from a payment on account | `advance` |
| A deposit taken on the order | `deposit` |
| A credit memo or miscellaneous credit | `credit_memo`, with `receipt_id` NULL |

## Reversals

In the Spine a reversal is a new application row with the negative amount and
`reverses_application_id` set. SX.e edits rows instead: it flags the receipt, marks the
paid installments as reversed, and creates new open installments from them.

So the adapter writes what SX.e does not.

| SX.e does | The adapter writes |
|---|---|
| Sets `aret.reversedfl` on the check record | `reversed_at` on the receipt |
| Marks the paid installments as reversed and creates new ones | A negative application row for each, dated with the reversing journal |
| Creates unapplied cash on the right customer, for a wrong-customer reversal | A new receipt for that customer |

Seeing a reversal means comparing each sync with the last. An application loaded
yesterday can be gone today.

## Check on the data

All three come from the tables. Pull the rows behind one cheque that paid two invoices
in full, one in part, and left a remainder, and the same payment after it was reversed.

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Which field says how a scheduled payment was closed? | On closed scheduled pay rows, which of `checknopaid`, `crjrnlno`, and `reversedfl` is set in each case. Compare with the customer's activity report |
| Does `paymtamt` include the discount? | Rows with `discamt` above zero: whether `amount` equals `paymtamt`, or `paymtamt` plus `discamt` |
| Does the journal number change when a scheduled payment is paid? | `jrnlno` on a closed scheduled pay row against `jrnlno` on its document row |
