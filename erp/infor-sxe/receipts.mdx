A Spine receipt is one incoming payment, with its unapplied remainder as a number on the
row. SX.e has no table for that. This page says what SX.e writes when money arrives and
how we assemble a `receipts` row from it.

## A receipt is a check record

In Spine terms, SX.e's check record is our `receipts` row. It is a row in `aret`, the same
table that holds invoices, with type code 7. It carries the cheque number, the amount,
the date, the customer, and the division. Everything else about the payment lives on
other rows: the invoices it paid are on their own rows, and the unapplied remainder is a
row of its own.

| Spine column | SX.e | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `customer_account_id` | `aret.custno` | |
| `business_unit_id` | `aret.divno` | Every payment has a division |
| `amount` | `aret.amount` | Store it positive. The stored sign is not confirmed |
| `currency` | `arsc.currencyty` on the customer | Blank means the company's currency |
| `receipt_date` | `aret.invdt` | SX.e reuses the invoice date column |
| `gl_date` | `aret.postdt` | |
| `bank_reference` | `aret.checkno`, or `aret.paymentrefer` when the cheque number is zero | Zero means cash, or a wire whose reference the clerk typed in |
| `maturity_date` | Nothing | SX.e has no post-dated cheque |
| `external_system_identifier` | `cono`, `custno`, `jrnlno`, `setno`, `checkno` | Or `aretp.rowpointer` when the payment record exists |

Every posting sits in a journal, `aret.jrnlno`, and a set inside it, `aret.setno`. The
journal is how SX.e groups the rows of one cash receipts session.

## The unapplied amount is a row, not a number

In the Spine, the part of a payment not yet matched to an invoice is `unapplied_amount`
on the receipt. SX.e writes it as a separate row in `aret`, type 3, with the invoice
number `0-00` and a sequence number.

Two consequences:
- Our `unapplied_amount` is the sum of the active unapplied cash rows that belong to the
  receipt.
- When part of it is later applied, SX.e closes the row and writes a new one for what is
  left, with the next sequence number. The remainder gets a new identity each time.

`on_account_amount` stays at 0. SX.e has no state for cash parked on purpose. A payment
nobody has matched and a payment left on account look the same.

## The optional payment record

In Spine terms, `aretp` is a second receipt header with a real foreign key from each
application. SX.e writes it only when **Allow AR Payment Reversals** is on, and only for
payments entered after it was switched on. Each paid invoice row then carries
`aret.payrowpointer` pointing at it.

Without it, the check record is the only header, and we find its applications by
matching values. The
[receipt applications page](/erp/infor-sxe/receipt-applications#finding-the-receipt)
covers both cases.

## Rows that look like receipts and are not

Four kinds of SX.e row would become a receipt if loaded naively.

| SX.e row | In Spine terms | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A Miscellaneous Cash posting | Not a receipt. It touches no customer balance | Leave it out |
| A deposit tendered on an order not yet invoiced | A liability, not a receipt yet | Leave it out until the order is invoiced. It then becomes an application with `funding_source = deposit` |
| A payment on a lockbox dummy customer | A receipt with no account | Load it with `customer_account_id` NULL |
| An invoice on a customer record that stands for a card processor | A debtor we must not chase | Exclude those accounts from outreach. It happens when a payment type has **Post to A/R at Invoicing** on |

## payment_method

SX.e stores no fixed payment method. The customer defines up to 99 payment type codes,
each with flags such as "is a credit card", and the field only appears when **Allow
Payment Types** is on. Ask for their payment type table. Without it:

| Cheque number | Payment reference | `payment_method` |
|---|---|---|
| Above zero | | `cheque` |
| Zero | Filled | `bank_transfer` |
| Zero | Blank | `cash`, if the customer confirms it |

## reversed_at

In the Spine a reversed receipt keeps its row with `reversed_at` set. SX.e does the
same: Customer Payment Reversal Entry (ARECR) sets `aret.reversedfl` on the check record
and makes the invoices it paid active again. It writes no offsetting row, so the
[reversal rows on the applications](/erp/infor-sxe/receipt-applications#reversals) are
ours to write.

`reversal_reason` is `nsf` for NSF Check, and `error` for the two wrong-application
reasons. SX.e has no stop-payment reason.

ARECR cannot reverse a payment taken in sales order entry or an **Apply Credits**
posting. A clerk corrects those by hand, and the correction looks like ordinary new rows.

## Check on the data

The first two decide how receipts are assembled, and both are visible in the tables.

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Is **Allow AR Payment Reversals** on, and since when? | Whether `aretp` has rows, and the earliest `aretp.postdttz` |
| One check record per cheque, or one per invoice paid? | A journal and set with several paid rows: count the type code 7 rows in it |
| How are ACH and wire receipts recorded? | Check records with `checkno` of zero and a filled `paymentrefer` |
