In the Spine a customer is three levels: a `customers` party, a `customer_accounts`
account that AR transacts on, and `customer_sites` with their uses. People are
`contacts` with a responsibility on the account. SX.e has one customer record that is
the party, the account, and the bill-to site at once, with ship-tos and contacts hanging
off it.

## One SX.e customer becomes

Take customer 10450, a plumbing contractor with two jobs set up as ship-tos and one
accounts payable clerk. In SX.e that is one `arsc` row, two `arss` rows, and one
`contacts` row. In the Spine it becomes:

| Spine row | How many | From |
|---|---|---|
| `customers` | 1 | The `arsc` row |
| `customer_accounts` | 1, `account_number` 10450 | The same `arsc` row |
| `customer_sites` | 3 | The `arsc` address as the primary site, and one per `arss` row |
| `site_uses` | `bill_to` on the primary site, `ship_to` on the two jobs | |
| `contacts` | 1 | The `contacts` row, through its link to customer 10450 |
| `contact_assignments` | 1, `dunning` | Derived. See [who to chase](#who-to-chase) |

Put the company number in every identifier, because two companies can use the same
customer number.

| Spine column | SX.e | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `customers.name` | `arsc.name` | |
| `customers.tax_id` | `arsc.gstreg` | A GST or VAT registration. SX.e has no general tax id |
| `customer_accounts.account_number` | `arsc.custno` | As text |
| `customer_accounts.payment_terms` | `arsc.termstype` | The description is in `sasta` where `codeiden` is `T` |
| `customer_accounts.credit_limit` | `arsc.credlim` | Zero means no limit. Store it as NULL |
| `customer_accounts.customer_class` | `arsc.custtype` | `arsc.class` is a profitability rank and is the wrong source |
| `customer_accounts.status` | `arsc.statustype` | Active or inactive |
| `customer_accounts.credit_hold` | `arsc.selltype` | Derived. See below |
| `site_uses`, `statements` | `arsc.statementty` | When the value is `O` |
| `external_system_identifier` | `cono`, `custno` | `{cono}:{custno}` |

## credit_hold is derived

The Spine has one boolean. SX.e stores no hold flag. It decides a hold at order entry
from a status code, the aging, and the credit limit, and only the status is stored, in
`arsc.selltype`.

| Code | SX.e meaning | `credit_hold` |
|---|---|---|
| `Y` | Orders allowed | False |
| `N` | No orders. SX.e calls this credit hold | True |
| `C`, `D` | Cash only, COD only | False |
| `H` | Hold until the date in `arsc.statusdt` | True before the date |
| `O` | Open until the date in `arsc.statusdt` | True after the date |

An example. Customer 10450 has `selltype` of `H` and `statusdt` of 15 October. Their
`credit_hold` is true until 15 October and false from then on, so the adapter has to
re-derive it each night. Infor documents every value. The code `C` is not in Infor's
documentation and comes from a public field dictionary.

The other two reasons SX.e holds orders, a balance in an old aging bucket and a balance
over the limit, Spinal works out from its own aging.

## A ship-to is a site that behaves like an account

In the Spine a site is an address with purposes, and the credit settings live on the
account. An SX.e ship-to, `arss`, is an address that can also carry its own terms,
credit limit, hold period, and stored balances. Construction distributors set up each
job as a ship-to, with the lien filing deadline on it.

An example. Customer 10450's job "Riverside Tower" is ship-to `RIV1` with a credit limit
of 25,000 and a lien deadline 90 days after the first sale. In the Spine it is a
`customer_sites` row with `erp_site_code` of `RIV1`, a `site_uses` of `ship_to`, and a
`customer_collection_profiles` row with `site_use_id` set and
`customer_credit_limits.overall_credit_limit` of 25,000. The lien deadline has no
column.

A document carries its ship-to in `aret.shipto`, and blank means the customer's own
site. Keep inactive ship-tos, because open documents can still point at them.

## Contacts have no responsibility

In the Spine a contact has a responsibility on an account, and `dunning` is how we know
who to chase. SX.e contacts are people with no role. They sit in `contacts`, link to
customers through `contacts-roles`, and keep their emails and phones in
`contacts-methods`.

| Spine column | SX.e |
|---|---|
| `contacts` row | A `contacts-roles` row with `roletype` of `arsc` or `arss` and `primarykey` of the customer number |
| `contacts.name` | `contacts.firstnm` and `contacts.lastnm` |
| `contacts.email`, `contacts.phone` | The `contacts-methods` row with `primaryfl` set |
| `contacts.do_not_email`, `contacts.do_not_call` | `contacts.donotemailfl`, `contacts.donotcallfl` |

An example. One accounts payable clerk is linked to three customers in SX.e. In the
Spine she is three `contacts` rows, one per party, because a Spine contact belongs to
one party. Her work email, the `contacts-methods` row with `primaryfl` set, fills
`contacts.email` on all three.

## Who to chase

The **Dunning** check box on the customer, `arsc.dunningfl`, is information only. Infor
says no report uses it. So the `dunning` responsibility is assigned by rule, taking the
first that finds a contact.

1. The email addresses the customer selected for statements. They are probably in
   `email-links`, whose columns are not documented.
2. Contacts whose `contacts.contacttype` the customer says means accounts payable.
3. The **Vendor Manager** on the customer record, `arsc.apmgr`. Infor describes this
   person as the one to contact about credit problems. It has a phone and no email.
4. The customer's general email, `arsc.email`.

An example. Customer 10450 emails statements to `ap@contractor.example`, has one
contact with type `AP`, and has "J. Smith" as vendor manager. Rule 1 finds the statement
address, so it becomes the `dunning` contact and the other two rules are not used. If
no rule finds anyone, leave the account without a dunning contact so it shows as a data
gap.

## Links the Spine cannot hold

SX.e has three links from one customer record to another. The Spine has no
account-to-account column.

- **Statements to Customer.** Three branches are three customers, each with its own
  invoices, and head office is a fourth. Each branch's record points at head office, so
  head office receives one statement for all three, and a credit hold on head office
  holds the branches too. In Spine terms, the debt sits on the branch account but the
  people to chase sit on head office's. The workaround is to attach head office's
  people to each branch account as its `dunning` contacts.
- **Invoice To.** A dealer's goods are billed to a finance company under a floor plan.
  This works as it does for Oracle Fusion: the invoice's `customer_account_id` is the
  finance company, and we chase the finance company. The dealer is not kept. The
  columns are `arsc.custno2` and `arsc.fpcustno`, and sources disagree on which is
  which.
- **Customer master group**, `arsc.groupid`. Several customers share one credit limit.
  Each account looks fine on its own while the group is over its limit.

## Check on the data

These come from the tables once we have access.

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Do they use ship-tos as jobs? | `arss` rows with `jobdesc` or the lien fields filled |
| Who receives statements, and how? | `arsc.estmttype`, and the rows in `email-links` |
| Which contact types exist? | `sasta` where `codeiden` is `CT`, with their descriptions |
| Do they use **Statements to Customer**, **Invoice To**, or groups? | Counts of non-blank `arsc.custno2`, `arsc.fpcustno`, and `arsc.groupid` |
