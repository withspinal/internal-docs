In the Spine, a group holds clients, each client is a legal entity with a ledger and at
least one business unit, and every AR row carries its client and business unit. SX.e
has two levels that can fill that tree: the company and the division. Which one fills
which Spine level depends on how the customer uses them.

## The three shapes

Take a distributor with a head office and two branches. SX.e lets them set that up three
ways, and each gives a different Spine tree.

| SX.e setup | Spine clients | Spine business units | Ledgers | Accounts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One company, branches as divisions | One | Division 10, division 20, and a default | One | Shared across the business units |
| One company, legal entities as divisions | One per division | One default each | One, shared by all clients | Shared across the clients |
| One company per legal entity | One per company | One default each | One per client | Duplicated per company, with nothing linking them |

The first two are easy. The third is awkward, because the Spine's account is shared
across clients and SX.e gives us two unrelated customer records. Finding out which shape
the customer has is
[the most important discovery question](/erp/infor-sxe/discovery-questions#the-most-important-question).

## The company is the client and the ledger

An SX.e company, `cono`, has its own legal name, tax ids, currency, fiscal calendar,
chart of accounts, operators, and customers. Every table carries the company number,
and every key starts with it. In Spine terms, one company is one `clients` row and one
`ledgers` row.

| Spine column | SX.e |
|---|---|
| `clients.erp_legal_entity_code` | `sasc.cono` |
| `clients.name` | `sasc.name` |
| `ledgers.erp_ledger_id` | `sasc.cono`. SX.e has one set of books per company and no ledger id |
| `ledgers.currency_code` | `sasc.currencyty`, a two-character code to translate to ISO |

Test and training companies share the database, so ask which company numbers are live.

## The division is the business unit

In the Spine, the business unit is the stripe on every AR row that decides who may see
it. SX.e's division, `divno`, is that stripe. It is on every invoice and every payment,
SX.e's own reports total by it, and SX.e restricts operators by it.

| Spine row | Where SX.e puts the division |
|---|---|
| `ar_transactions` | `aret.divno`. Blank unless the company's **Divisional** option is on |
| `receipts` | `aret.divno`. Always set |
| `customer_accounts` | `arsc.divno`. The customer's home division. Can be blank |

An example. A customer's home division is 10. They buy from a warehouse in division
20, and the invoice posts with division 20. In the Spine that invoice belongs to
business unit 20, not 10, so read the division from the row and never from the account.
A row with a blank division goes to the client's default business unit.

The warehouse is not a candidate for the business unit. A payment and a transaction
typed into AR carry no warehouse.

## When the division is the client

Some distributors run several legal entities as divisions of one company, with each
division carrying its own tax id and address, in `sastn.fedtaxid` and `sastn.addr`.
In Spine terms each division is then a client with one default business unit, and all
of them share the company's ledger.

An invoice in division 20 then lands as `client_id` of the division 20 client and
`business_unit_id` of its default business unit. The customer record stays shared,
which is the Spine's own shape. For this to work, every AR row needs a division, so the
company's **Divisional** option has to be on.

## Accounts are shared within a company only

In the Spine an account belongs to the group and is shared by every client. SX.e shares
a customer record across divisions, which matches, and never across companies. The same
head office set up in company 1 and company 2 is two `arsc` rows, each with its own
balance, and they may not even share a customer number. Put `cono` in every account
identifier.

## A document's currency comes from the account

In the Spine every document carries its currency. In SX.e a document has no currency
column. It is in its customer's currency, `arsc.currencyty`, or the sales order's,
`oeeh.currencyty`, and blank means the company's currency.

An example. A company keeps its books in USD. A customer is set to CAD. Their invoice has
`aret.amount` of 1,000, meaning CAD, and `aret.exchgrate` of 0.74. In the Spine that is
`original_amount` 1,000 in CAD and `accounted_amount` 740 in the ledger's USD.

Two traps affect `accounted_amount`. Changing a customer's currency does not convert
their existing documents. The revaluation report, run in update mode, overwrites the
rate on open documents.

A company is single-currency when the currency table, `sastc`, is empty. Most
distributors are.

## gl_date is the posting date

In the Spine a document has a `transaction_date` and a `gl_date`. SX.e has the same
pair: `aret.invdt` and `aret.postdt`. An invoice dated 30 September but posted in a
journal opened on 2 October has `transaction_date` 30 September and `gl_date` 2
October, and SX.e's trial balance as of 30 September leaves it out.

SX.e has no single AR control account. A company can have several, and the Customer
Trial Balance Report prints each. The
[reconciliation page](/erp/infor-sxe/reconciliation) covers the tie.

## tax_amount is four amounts

The Spine holds one `tax_amount` per document and per line. SX.e holds four per
document, in `oeeh.taxamt`, and four per line, in `oeel.taxamount`: state, county, city,
and other. Sum them. Jurisdiction codes and rates are in `arett`, one row per order,
and with a third-party tax engine Infor says tax reporting is done in the engine.

## Access is granted per division

In the Spine a user is granted business units, and the set becomes their
`VisibleScope`. SX.e grants an operator a list of allowed divisions, switched on per
module by **Restrict Data Access For AR**.

An example. An operator has allowed divisions 10 and 20 and the AR switch on. In the
Spine they get grants on business unit 10, business unit 20, and the default business
unit, because SX.e shows rows with a blank division to every operator.

An operator record, `sasoo`, is per company. A person in two companies has two, linked by
the **IFS User** field. The column that holds the allowed divisions is not confirmed.

## Check on the data

These come from the tables once we have access.

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| How many companies, and which are live? | Rows in `sasc`, and which company numbers have recent rows in `aret` |
| Are divisions on AR rows? | Whether `aret.divno` is ever blank |
| Do divisions carry tax ids? | `sastn.fedtaxid` on the division list |
| Any foreign currency? | Rows in `sastc`, and non-blank `arsc.currencyty` |
| Any restricted operators? | `sasoo`, once the allowed-divisions column is confirmed |
